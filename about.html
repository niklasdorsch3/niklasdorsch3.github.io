Niklas Dorsch paints figures, scenes, and landscapes suspended in a moment extracted from a larger, unseen story. Humans, animals, and creatures occupy interiors and village streets that feel arrested rather than staged. What happened before, and what happens next, is withheld.

The figures are not planned. They are invented in the act of painting, discovered across sessions, and often re-invented — a face changes and the whole reading of the picture changes with it. They come out displaced, never quite belonging to the rooms they stand in. Dorsch is a Grenzgänger, a German-American on the border of two cultures and fully inside neither, and the figures carry that condition in their bodies.

The paintings are thickly built, with visible brushwork accumulating across the surface. Ochres and browns are set against saturated yellows, blues, and reds. Faces take on a sculptural, mask-like quality that heightens emotional pressure. Dorsch has studied at the New York Studio School and the Art Students League, and is based in Brooklyn, NY.
