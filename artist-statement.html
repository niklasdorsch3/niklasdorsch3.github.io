Niklas Dorsch paints figures, scenes and landscapes suspended in psychological and physical states that hint at the supernatural nature of the paint. Humans, animals and creatures occupy a moment extracted from a larger, unseen story.

His work sits within a contemporary figuration tradition that traces back through raw expressionism and post-impressionist flattened, emotionally-charged space.

Thickly built paintings carry visible brushwork accumulating across the surface. Ochres and browns set against saturated yellows, blues, reds grind a soulful grit into the narrative. Faces take on a sculptural, mask-like quality that heightens emotional pressure.

Hidden tensions bind the work: between bodies and their environments, between intention and collapse, between what is shown and what remains obscured. A gothic quality runs through this investigation — a searching for meaning in psychological discomfort rather than beauty or resolution.

He has studied at the New York Studio School and the Art Students League, and is based in Brooklyn, NY.
