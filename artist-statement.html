Niklas Dorsch paints figures suspended in psychological and physical states that resist easy resolution. His work sits within a contemporary figuration tradition that traces back through Schiele's raw expressionism and Gauguin's flattened, emotionally-charged space — a lineage concerned with the body as a carrier of interior states rather than surface information.

His paintings are built thickly, with visible brushwork that accumulates across the surface. Color arrives suddenly: ochres and browns fractured by saturated yellows, blues, reds that feel almost violent against muted grounds. Faces carry a sculptural, sometimes mask-like quality that heightens emotional pressure without resolving it. The work resists narrative closure — each painting feels like a moment extracted from a larger, unseen story.

What binds the work is an interest in hidden tensions: between bodies and their environments, between intention and collapse, between what is shown and what remains obscured. There is a gothic quality to this investigation — a searching for meaning in psychological discomfort rather than beauty or resolution. The paintings are soulful in their grit, physical in their resistance to polish.

Dorsch has studied at the New York Studio School and the Art Students League, and is based in Brooklyn, NY.
